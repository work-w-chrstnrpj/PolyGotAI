# English Foundations: grammar sequence and learning acceptance

> v2 (2026-10-25): this grammar sequence feeds the CEFR course and the [Grammarian coach track](coach-tracks.md#1-grammarian--owl-grammarian-artwork-exists). Acceptance thresholds below are teaching targets, not app gates. Ticket references are history.

> Owner adopted 2026-10-19 (Asia/Manila). Required product/curriculum coverage, not authored lessons or implementation. Level placements, examples and initial assessment thresholds require qualified educator review before publication. All execution evidence is NOT_RUN.

## Purpose and entry paths

Teach how English works, from word types to connected sentences, and connect each concept to useful communication. English Foundations lives within Learn, accessible through focused grammar practice and contextual links from CEFR lessons and feedback. It is not a separate navigation tab, mandatory placement course or prerequisite lock. Learners can skip familiar explanations, revisit a foundation and return to their current lesson; actually available levels remain freely selectable. Planned foundations stay visibly unavailable until reviewed content/assets exist.

Foundations and CEFR lessons reference shared versioned grammar objectives, examples and evidence rather than maintaining duplicate courses or completion records. A learner may understand a grammar label yet struggle to use the structure, or use it well without remembering its label. Show those results separately; terminology is not a requirement for passing a communicative task.

## Adopted teaching sequence

The sequence below guides recommendations and authoring; it does not require every strand to be completed before speaking. Introduce a simple use early and revisit it with greater complexity. IDs identify strands; authors assign stable child objective IDs for the specific forms/usages they teach.

| Strand | Explicit required coverage | Practical learning outcome | Initial lesson connections / planned progression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EF-01 · Word types | Nouns: common/proper, singular/plural, countable/uncountable; pronouns: subject/object, possessive and reflexive forms; verbs: main/linking/auxiliary; adjectives; adverbs; determiners including articles, demonstratives and quantifiers; prepositions; coordinating/subordinating conjunctions; common interjections | Choose words that identify a person/thing, describe it, express an action/state and connect ideas; recognize that word class depends on use in context | A1 introductions, family, objects and routines; A2 services/travel and fuller pronoun/quantifier use; later refinement in connected discourse |
| EF-02 · Sentence structure | Subject; predicate (including the verb and its dependents); direct/indirect objects; subject/object complements where appropriate; basic word order; statements, questions, negatives and imperatives; simple/compound/complex sentences; sentence boundaries, capitalization and punctuation; fragments and run-ons | Build a complete intended message, ask a question, make a negative and repair a confusing sentence without changing its meaning | A1 introductions/basic requests; A2 arrangements and explanations; B1+ stories, opinions and longer messages |
| EF-03 · Agreement and verb forms | Subject–verb agreement with be, have and simple present; third-person singular endings; do/does and base verb in questions/negatives; pronoun reference/agreement; singular/plural and there is/are; later agreement with coordinated subjects, intervening phrases and quantity expressions | Say/write who does what using an appropriate verb form, and explain/correct a mismatch in context | A1 self/family introductions and routines; A2 descriptions/quantities; B1–C1 increasingly complex subjects and reference, with valid variety differences accepted |
| EF-04 · Phrases and clauses | Noun, verb, adjective, adverb and prepositional phrases; head and modifiers at suitable depth; finite clause basics and subject/predicate roles; independent/dependent clauses; coordination/subordination; relative, adverbial and noun/content clauses; nonfinite constructions at later levels | Expand a short message with useful detail and join ideas while preserving who did what and the intended relationship | A1 simple phrases/clauses; A2 place/time phrases and basic because/when/if connections; B1 relative/content clauses and connected explanations; B2+ more complex clause relationships and later nonfinite structures |
| EF-05 · Progressive grammar in use | Tense/aspect: present/past/future reference, simple/continuous/perfect combinations; questions, negatives and auxiliaries revisited; modals; comparisons; gerunds/infinitives; conditionals; passive voice; reported speech; relative clauses revisited; emphasis/inversion, cohesion, hedging and register at later levels | Describe routines/events/plans, compare, give advice, explain possibilities and convey other people's ideas with increasing precision | A1–A2 everyday exchanges; B1 stories/opinions; B2 detailed arguments; C1–C2 flexible professional/academic expression and nuanced reformulation |

This is explicit required coverage, not an exhaustive inventory of every exception or an official CEFR grammar syllabus. SC-T006 must turn it into a reviewed child-objective coverage matrix; examples of pending details include exact lesson quantities, sequencing of particular pronoun forms and treatment of variety-sensitive agreement. Do not present the entire EF-04/EF-05 inventory as A1 prerequisites. Use simple, reviewed examples before technical depth. In the foundational teaching convention, distinguish a word's class from its sentence function: a pronoun can serve as a subject, and a noun phrase can serve as an object. A verb is part of a predicate; predicate does not mean object. Explicitly teach that imperatives can have an understood subject rather than marking them incomplete.

## CEFR connection and authoring contract

CEFR describes language abilities across languages; language-specific curriculum designers select grammatical content. The [Council of Europe explanation](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/reference-level-descriptions) and [British Council–EAQUALS inventory](https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/publications/case-studies-insights-and-research/british-council-eaquals-core-inventory-general) support using a separate English-content mapping. Our placements are planning choices, not official rules that a grammar topic belongs exclusively to a level. C2 develops flexible control rather than requiring obscure terminology.

Each published foundation child objective must record:

- Stable grammar objective ID/version, parent EF strand, exact structure/use and prerequisite recommendations.
- Related CEFR unit/topic/lesson IDs, practical can-do outcome, descriptor scale/reference and mapping rationale, with educator approval. Pending descriptor references must remain explicitly unresolved and block publication; a level label alone is not a reviewed mapping.
- Plain-English explanation, original/permitted text/reference assets, valid alternatives, common errors and editorial provenance. Accept valid English variants while teaching American English defaults.
- Separate concept-identification, sentence-production and communicative-use tasks; answer/rubric versions; target opportunities; assessment mode/support; unseen comparable delayed/transfer variants.
- Core completion rule, evidence availability, offline fallback and next-practice links, with explicit vocabulary-review credit policy (initially explicit rated reviews only).

Link both ways: a CEFR lesson offers a relevant foundation refresher; the foundation offers an appropriate CEFR application task. Do not create an all-or-nothing grammar prerequisite or infer reading/listening/speaking proficiency from a terminology quiz.

## First connection: INTRO-01

Deliver a bounded subset through SC-T018/SC-T020 before expanding the sequence:

| Foundation child objective | Teaching/example | Evidence in Introductions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EF-01-NOUN-01 / EF-01-PRON-01 | Names/roles and subject pronouns: Alex, teacher, I, she | Identify noun/pronoun use in reviewed examples; choose a suitable subject without assuming every noun is a subject |
| EF-02-SP-01 | In "She works as a teacher", She is the subject and works as a teacher is the predicate; "She is a teacher" uses a subject complement | Identify subject/predicate and build an original complete introduction sentence; do not mislabel the complement as an object |
| EF-03-BE-01 | I am, she is, they are; I am not; Are you…? | Two of the existing five controlled grammar items assess be with reviewed subject contrasts |
| EF-03-PRES-01 | I work / she works; She does not work; Does she work…? | Two existing items assess simple-present agreement; the fifth assesses a question/negative with appropriate auxiliary/base form |

Integrate plain-language teaching and examples into the existing pack. The optional five-item concept check samples noun/pronoun distinction, subject/predicate roles and explaining one agreement correction; it is diagnostic and separate from INTRO-01 core completion. The five existing grammar items remain five; freeze the chosen form/answer coverage during authoring. First-response evidence and later retries remain distinct. The mixed five-item INTRO-01 pack supports its topic-level controlled grammar check; it cannot establish a 4/5 result for each separate child objective. A per-child EF-AC02/03 result requires its own reviewed five-item set, offered as separate foundation assessment without enlarging INTRO-01 core completion.

INTRO-01's independent three-fact self-introduction and follow-up remain unchanged. To elicit third-person agreement, a foundation practice/application variant can ask the learner to introduce a fictional colleague; label that a separate task with reviewed difficulty. Do not require third-person performance in a self-only prompt or count an absent target opportunity as an error. The full object/complement, phrase and clause sequence is authored later rather than expanding the first internal topic into a complete grammar course.

## Learning acceptance criteria

Initial numeric thresholds below are local starting criteria requiring educator review, not validated mastery/CEFR cutoffs. They do not replace the INTRO-01 rubric, vocabulary-retention checks or completion rule.

| ID | Required evidence / acceptance boundary |
| --- | --- |
| EF-AC01 · Coverage and mapping | Every published child objective has the authoring metadata above and a reviewed linked CEFR task; every required strand/topic is present in the publication coverage matrix as reviewed/available or explicitly pending. A complete-release claim requires no unaccounted required grammar topic. |
| EF-AC02 · Concept understanding | A bounded objective has five reviewed unseen identification/explanation items. At least 4/5 unaided first responses supports a local "concept understood" result; record misidentifications, assistance and valid explanations separately from productive use. Terminology failure cannot automatically fail communication. |
| EF-AC03 · Controlled production | Five fresh sentence-building/correction responses with defined target opportunities; at least 4/5 correct unaided first responses supports controlled-use evidence. A multiple-choice answer or memorized model cannot substitute for this check. |
| EF-AC04 · Communicative use | A related unfamiliar task elicits the target structure in at least two reviewed natural opportunities. Learner conveys the intended message and uses the target appropriately in both without model/hints/correction. Target avoidance or too few opportunities means not assessed, not a pass/fail; use a reviewed follow-up to elicit the form. An effective alternative can pass communication while leaving that specific form unassessed. |
| EF-AC05 · Retention and transfer | Repeat a fresh five-response production check around day 7 (±2) and day 28 (±3) from initial teaching, before answers/review; require at least 4/5 at each for delayed controlled-use evidence. Report actual practice/delay. Separately apply EF-AC04 to a new comparable situation. Do not infer retained communicative use from a repeated worksheet. |
| EF-AC06 · Completion and access | For a standalone foundation lesson, attempt the five concept and five production items, review corrections for misses, and attempt one linked applied task plus self-review. Wrong answers after review do not block completion. Quiet/text completion is valid with speaking unassessed; no key/network, gold, terminology pass or retained private recording is required. Contextual refreshers do not silently enlarge CEFR lesson completion. |
| EF-AC07 · Shared evidence | Foundation and CEFR entry points share objective/version and source-attempt evidence. Keep concept understanding, controlled production, communication, delayed use, assistance and modality separate. No duplicate rewards/review credits and no automatic CEFR promotion or mastery from grammar labels/XP. |
| EF-AC08 · Review and recovery | Educator reviews content, target opportunities, descriptor mapping, alternatives and thresholds before assessment; offline/restart/incorrect-answer and decline/delete paths preserve truthful evidence. Unsupported pronunciation, missing evidence or edited transcripts never establish sound-level ability. |

Show observed strengths and needs per objective. When a learner can identify a rule but cannot use it, recommend sentence construction and contextual practice with a reason; when communication succeeds but terminology is weak, offer an optional refresher rather than demoting the learner. Preserve privacy, voluntary assessment and independent audio/text retention.
