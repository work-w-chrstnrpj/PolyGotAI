# American English Curriculum Outline: A1–C2

> v2 (2026-10-25): this is the topic outline for the **CEFR course** in Learn. Lessons now use the auto-checked [exercise engine](../tdd/tdd.md#exercise-engine) and start with a coach tip. Specialist skills (debate, journalism, copywriting, …) live in the [coach tracks](coach-tracks.md), not here. Sections about v1 trainers, tickets and publication gates are history.

## Scope and interpretation

American English is the default for spelling, vocabulary, examples and reference audio; accept valid alternatives. The levels organize proposed learning topics around communication outcomes. Exact topic placement, prerequisites, lesson order, coverage and assessment mappings need qualified editorial review. Topics recur with increasing complexity; their appearance in a level does not mean exclusive ownership by that level or guaranteed mastery after one lesson.

CEFR describes abilities rather than prescribing this grammar/topic sequence. The British Council–EAQUALS Core Inventory is a planning reference through A1–C1; the C2 outline is a project proposal informed by CEFR outcomes, not an extension claimed to come from that inventory. C2 emphasizes integrated precision and flexibility rather than collecting obscure grammar rules. No level implies mandatory native-like accent or official certification.

Introductions at A1 remains the first internal authoring/proof target. Owner UI/UX decisions now require a complete A1–C2 five-skill English path with interview/business coverage for public release. Exact volume and completeness criteria remain open; this outline is not reviewed/published curriculum or authorization to begin application implementation. Learners choose any available level freely; unreleased content is marked Coming later.

| Vocabulary | Personal details; common objects; everyday verbs; basic adjectives; useful requests and phrases |
| Speaking | Introduce yourself; describe your family; order food; ask prices; describe your routine; ask for help |
| Pronunciation | Common sounds in familiar words; word stress; basic sentence stress; intelligible short phrases |
| Communication | Express one clear idea; ask someone to repeat; answer simple questions; take turns |

## Cross-level lesson design

Each unit connects learning objective → grammar → vocabulary → listening/reading example → speaking activity → feedback → scheduled review. Listening and reading support task understanding and broader proficiency; exact coverage is still to be specified. Add reviewed listening/reading comprehension criteria rather than assuming speaking performance demonstrates those skills.

For each authored lesson, specify stable ID/content version, descriptor reference and scale, communicative outcome, language targets, prerequisites, activity types, American English examples/reference audio, answer rules or rubric, assessment evidence, source/rights provenance and editorial state. Unit/topic/lesson breakdown and exercise quantities remain open. See the [technical design](../history/v1/tdd/tdd.md) for generation policies.

Communication techniques remain accessible across levels. A1 learners can give a simple headline-first answer; advanced learners can use it for an executive recommendation. PREP, STAR and other named frameworks are teaching aids, not CEFR requirements or level locks. See [communication training](../history/v1/product-specification/communication-training.md).

Use validated AI-generated variations within approved objectives and difficulty constraints, preserve Anki-style card identities, and retain repeatable/comparable assessment tasks. Listening, reading, speaking and language knowledge need appropriate evidence; a completion count or AI rating alone does not establish overall proficiency.

## Lesson structure and entry paths

Curriculum hierarchy: English → CEFR level → Unit → Topic → Lesson → Activities. Activities belong within a lesson; learners do not need to finish all teaching before reaching a separate exercise section. Default approach: short teaching segments interleaved with supported practice, then applied speaking, feedback and later review. The learning objective remains stable while approved activity variants change.

| Step | Learner experience |
| --- | --- |
| Set the goal | Understand the practical communication outcome |
| Learn through examples | Read/listen to an example and a concise explanation |
| Learn useful language | Explore relevant grammar, vocabulary and phrases |
| Practice with support | Complete/correct sentences, construct answers or use scaffolds |
| Use it in speech | Record an objective-linked response with optional support |
| Review and retry | Replay/self-review offline, request AI feedback online and optionally retry |
| Remember and transfer | Review selected vocabulary and later apply the objective in a related new situation |

This is a flexible design pattern, not seven mandatory screens or a fixed activity count. Grammar lessons can emphasize sentence construction; communication lessons can lead into a recorded answer. Speaking remains optional for vocabulary practice. Exact duration, activity counts, completion rules and speaking requirements by lesson type need design. Offline core study and self-review must not depend on personalized cloud feedback.

Entry paths: guided CEFR progression; focused grammar/vocabulary/speaking/communication practice; and review of difficult material. Speech Lab is independently accessible. Curriculum prerequisites guide recommendations without preventing the learner from selecting actually available levels. Completion records participation/task completion; lasting improvement needs later recall and comparable practice.

### Example lesson

A1 → Everyday introductions → Personal information → Introduce yourself. Goal: say who you are and where you live. Examples introduce “I am…” and “I live…”, with occupations, places and interests. Supported sentence activities lead to an optional scaffolded introduction recording, feedback/self-review and retry. Later transfer: introduce yourself to a new colleague. This illustrates authoring, not a published lesson or finalized CEFR mapping.

## Embedded vocabulary and Vocabulary Builder

CEFR lessons teach vocabulary in context alongside grammar and speaking. For an A1 ordering-food task, candidate items include “menu”, “water” and “I’d like…”. Explain meanings, demonstrate usage and reuse them in sentence activities and linked speaking practice. Vocabulary Builder remains independently available for daily Anki-style review, personal words and custom topic sets.

Learners may opt to add lesson expressions to their review collection. Use shared vocabulary identities by language/variety and word sense; multiple lessons can reference an item without creating duplicate review cards. Shared learning evidence preserves source, task and assistance context. Recognition and productive use remain separate; seeing a word or completing a lesson does not count as successful scheduled recall.

Lesson attempts may supply vocabulary learning evidence, but the approved initial rule is that only explicit rated reviews advance schedules; rating implementation, duplicate detection and card-suggestion defaults still need specification. Do not silently reset schedules or count one answer as multiple reviews. Personal wording/edits and different word senses need explicit reconciliation rules before implementation.

## English Foundations coverage and CEFR connections

Owner adopted the [English Foundations sequence](english-foundations.md) on 2026-10-19. EF-01 word types → EF-02 sentence structure → EF-03 agreement/verb forms → EF-04 phrases/clauses → EF-05 progressive grammar is an authoring/recommendation sequence with increasingly complex revisits, not a compulsory course before speaking. Foundations are available through focused Learn practice and contextual CEFR lesson links, sharing child objective IDs/progress.

| CEFR planning stage | Foundation application | Example communicative lesson connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Basic word classes, subject/predicate, simple objects/complements, be/present agreement, question/negative order and simple phrases/clauses | Introduce yourself; describe a family member; describe a routine; request an item |
| A2 | Fuller pronoun/reference and quantity use, phrase expansion, simple linked clauses and continued agreement | Make arrangements; describe a past event; explain a familiar problem |
| B1 | Relative/adverbial/content clauses, more complex subjects, tense relationships and reported messages | Tell a connected story; explain a decision; describe a project |
| B2 | More complex clauses, conditional/passive/reported structures and precise connections | Defend a recommendation; explain technical concepts; negotiate |
| C1 | Flexible complex syntax, nonfinite constructions at suitable depth, reference/cohesion, emphasis/hedging/register | Present trade-offs; summarize a report; handle demanding questions |
| C2 | Integrated precision, reformulation, nuance and flexible grammatical choices | Resolve ambiguity; synthesize arguments; adapt meaning/style to an audience |

These are project planning links, not official topic-to-level mandates. Before publication, SC-T006 requires a child-objective coverage matrix with exact CEFR descriptor scale/reference, lesson IDs, target structures, recommended prerequisites, reviewed task/answer criteria and educator sign-off. Mark unresolved mappings explicitly; do not use a level label or outline as proof of alignment. Every required EF topic must have an accountable delivery location before release completeness can close.

Each linked lesson separates concept understanding, controlled production and communicative use, followed by fresh delayed/transfer tasks using EF-AC01–EF-AC08. Initial thresholds require educator review; concept-name recall alone never establishes language ability. [Introductions](../history/v1/product-specification/introductions-learning-loop.md) incorporates its specified noun/pronoun, subject/predicate, be and simple-present subset without expanding its core activity count or requiring all foundations first.

## Specialized trainers connected to curriculum (v1, superseded by coach tracks)

The [seven adopted trainers](../history/v1/product-specification/specialized-trainers.md) share CEFR/EF objectives but teach through different reviewed methods. Teacher connects foundations to guided/faded application; Grammarian drills accurate construction then transfer; Librarian connects comprehension and productive recall; Journalist trains questions, relevant follow-ups and source-faithful summary; Business Professional trains audience-aware workplace exchanges; Speaker trains supported audio/intelligibility targets; Creative Writer trains written expression and separately evidenced spoken storytelling.

Each authored pack maps primary and specialty objectives, descriptor references, method/task/rubric versions, level-appropriate activities, precise core completion, target opportunities and fresh/delayed assessment. No profession has a fixed CEFR level; A1 versions use simple reviewed language and advanced versions expand demands. Shared grammar/vocabulary progress does not erase distinct source-comprehension, delivery, workplace or storytelling evidence. The three early INTRO-01 trainer paths do not expand its mandatory core counts or alter independent assessment; all-seven course coverage is tracked by SC-T044 and audited by SC-T040. Apply [TR acceptance](../history/v1/testing/specialized-trainer-acceptance.md) alongside EF/IL criteria.
