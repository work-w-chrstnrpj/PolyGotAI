## Purpose and placement

Help learners organize ideas, speak clearly, listen actively and communicate appropriately in everyday, professional and public settings. Proposed placement: a Communication learning track in Lessons, linked to guided exercises in Speech Lab, relevant Vocabulary sets and evidence in Activity. A new tab is not required; final navigation awaits UI/UX design.

Communication skill complements English proficiency. Teach American English by default while accepting valid alternatives. Match language complexity to available CEFR content; using a framework does not itself establish a CEFR level. Beginners can organize simple ideas without being required to produce advanced business vocabulary.

| Framework | Candidate structure | Practice context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PREP | Point, Reason, Example, Point | Opinions and spontaneous answers |
| BLUF | Bottom Line Up Front | Concise recommendations and updates |
| STAR | Situation, Task, Action, Result | Experience-based interview answers |
| SCQA | Situation, Complication, Question, Answer | Explaining a problem and solution |
| SBI | Situation, Behavior, Impact | Specific, constructive feedback |
| PEEL | Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link | Supported arguments and presentations |

| 5W1H | Who, What, When, Where, Why, How | Information gathering and clear explanations |

Recommended initial teaching sequence: PREP, BLUF, STAR, SCQA, SBI. This is a product proposal reflecting the owner's examples, not an empirically established universal ranking. Remaining frameworks are expansion candidates. Explain when a framework helps and when an ordinary natural response is better; do not force every answer into an acronym.

## Skills beyond frameworks

Teach audience/purpose awareness, clear opening and closing, logical transitions, relevant examples, concise explanation, active listening and clarification, respectful disagreement, empathetic feedback, pacing, intentional pauses and vocal emphasis. Public-speaking practice may include a short prepared talk, an impromptu answer, a technical explanation, a pitch and audience questions. Teach nonverbal delivery through explanations and self-review only unless a future video capability is explicitly scoped.

## Connected learning loop

1. Explain a technique and show original, reviewed examples and contrasting responses.
2. Let the learner identify or arrange parts of an answer.
3. Offer a planning scaffold and topic matched to level, purpose and interests.
4. Learner records, replays and receives afterward feedback when connected.
5. Prioritize one or two actionable improvements, then optionally retry.
6. Gradually reduce scaffolding and try a new related scenario to explore transfer.

Framework, guided/free practice, preparation time, response duration, coaching style and feedback focus follow configurable learner preferences. Exact defaults remain undecided. Preserve a no-framework practice choice. Lessons/scaffolds and self-review work offline; fresh AI scenarios and personalized corrections require connectivity, with validated cached or reviewed fallback activities.

## Dynamic activity and feedback rules

AI can vary prompts, audiences, constraints and follow-up questions within approved objectives and rubrics. Apply the technical design's generation validation policy. Feedback distinguishes message organization/relevance, English grammar/vocabulary, supported audio delivery observations and learner-reported confidence. Cite the response excerpts behind suggestions; natural effective answers need not use the literal framework labels.

Transcripts support content feedback; audio is needed for pacing or other delivery observations. Do not infer eye contact, posture, internal emotions or confidence from audio/transcripts alone. Intentional pauses are not automatically errors, and speech differences must not become accent penalties. Technical correctness coaching remains optional. Hypothetical sample stories must be labeled as examples; never invent a learner's accomplishments or numerical results. Framework use is not proof of truth, expertise or communication success.

## Acceptance and evidence

Learner can learn a framework, practice with or without a scaffold, record/replay, receive differentiated feedback online and retry or choose a fresh task. Offline self-review remains usable. Comparisons preserve framework, objective, prompt difficulty and rubric version. Completion badges reward practice; improvement claims require comparable evidence and reviewed criteria. No universal articulation/confidence score is promised.

## Practical technique curriculum

Owner-requested expansion: teach the following techniques alongside frameworks. The attached owner notes supply the candidate catalog; explanatory claims, named methods and example wording require editorial/source review before publication. Techniques are flexible tools, not mandatory rules or guarantees of persuasive success.

| Learning family | Techniques to teach | Candidate practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clarity and organization | Rule of Three; One Idea, One Sentence; Headline First; Chunking; Signposting; Plain Language; Analogies; Progressive Disclosure; Strategic Pausing; Verbal Economy | Explain a technical idea to a beginner; lead with the message; shorten an answer without losing meaning |
| Conversation and listening | Active Listening; Paraphrasing; Mirroring; Open-ended Questions; Follow-up Questions; Validation; Conversational Turn-taking; Reflective Listening; Curiosity Before Judgment; Calibrated Questions | Listen/read a short response, summarize, clarify and ask a relevant follow-up |
| Reasoning and ethical persuasion | Ethos/Pathos/Logos; Framing; Steel-manning; Common Ground; Evidence-Based Claims; Contrast Principle; WIIFM; Inoculation; Socratic Questioning; Call to Action | Explain two options fairly; support a recommendation; acknowledge an objection and invite a next step |
| Delivery and composure | Brief pause before answering (the supplied Two-Second Pause); Vocal Variety; Filler Word Reduction; Natural Eye Contact; Think–Organize–Speak; Assertive Language | Prepare briefly, record, replay and retry with one delivery focus |
| Difficult conversations | I-Statements; Pause Before Reacting; Separate Facts from Interpretations; Clarify Before Disagreeing; Disagree Without Dismissing; Boundary Setting; De-escalation; Repair Attempts | Simulate a misunderstanding or disagreement; clarify, state needs and propose repair |

The Rule of Three and One Idea, One Sentence are heuristics; do not grade every answer against fixed counts. A brief preparation pause has no universal two-second requirement. Occasional fillers, thoughtful pauses and cautious wording may be appropriate. Keep eye-contact guidance culturally sensitive and optional; no video analysis is approved. Reflective listening should check possible feelings rather than assert knowledge of another person's emotions. Persuasion practice must preserve truthful evidence, uncertainty and respect for the listener's choice; avoid invented facts or pressure tactics.

## Teach within Speech Lab and Lessons

Proposed Speech Lab entry: Communication Practice. A learner chooses a skill and can open a short explanation, reviewed example and optional scaffold immediately before practicing. The same lesson remains available in the Lessons communication track. Both entry points reference one canonical lesson/objective and shared progress; they do not duplicate curriculum or force a tab change.

Candidate practice modes:

- Explain Clearly: plain language, chunking, analogies and signposting.
- Speak Concisely: headline-first answers, verbal economy and configurable timed summaries.
- Listen and Respond: paraphrasing, clarification, follow-ups and turn-taking.
- Present and Persuade: audience-aware talks, fair comparisons, supported arguments and next steps.
- Handle Difficult Conversations: respectful disagreement, boundaries and repair.
- Delivery Practice: preparation, pacing, vocal emphasis and intentional pauses.

Guided solo drills use recording with afterward feedback. Interactive listening/conversation drills follow the existing later live-conversation scope; offline reviewed audio/text exchanges can support basic listening practice before that capability exists. Teach first, practice with a scaffold, review focused evidence, retry, then use the skill in a fresh situation. Link vocabulary and grammar to each task where useful. Framework choice remains optional; techniques should become natural habits rather than rigid scripts.

## Optional daily routine

Offer a learner-configurable checklist: a PREP answer; a simple explanation; a concise summary (30 seconds as an adjustable example); paraphrasing before responding; intentional-pause practice; and constructive disagreement. Learners choose the items and workload rather than needing all six every day. Home can offer a shortcut; Activity tracks completion. Checklist completion earns participation credit, not automatic skill mastery. Detailed reminder and reset behavior remains to be designed.

## Progress dimensions and additional acceptance

Organize feedback around clarity, conciseness, coherence, expression/articulation, learner-reported confidence, empathetic response choices and quality of persuasive reasoning. These are learning dimensions, not an approved numeric mastery scale. Do not infer intelligence, personality, genuine empathy or internal confidence from a recording. Cite observable answer excerpts or supported audio evidence; collect optional learner reflection for confidence.

Acceptance: a learner can access a technique lesson from Lessons or Speech Lab, understand a reviewed example, perform a level-appropriate activity, receive supported feedback when online, and retry without being forced to use an acronym. Shared objective progress remains consistent across entry points. Offline instruction/self-review works. Everyday, interview, spontaneous and presentation activities can reuse these skills without changing release scope silently.
