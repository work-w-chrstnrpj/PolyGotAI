# Introductions learning loop and acceptance

> Adopted direction: 2026-10-19 (Asia/Manila). Product requirements for the first internal A1 topic; content, app, educator review and learner results remain NOT_RUN. Numeric criteria are initial pilot defaults requiring qualified educator review before use, not validated proficiency cutoffs or CEFR certification.

## Outcome and boundaries

Objective `INTRO-01`: introduce yourself to someone new, communicate three relevant facts (chosen name, work/study or daily role, and an interest), and answer one simple related follow-up without a script, model answer, word bank or corrective prompt. Fictional details are allowed; personal disclosure is never required. Accept simple A1 language and valid English varieties; American English remains the teaching/reference default.

Grammar targets: present-tense forms of be, simple present for work/study/interests, and simple related questions/answers. The educator must review prerequisite coverage, exact target phrases, examples, permitted alternatives and prompt difficulty. This bounded topic does not establish general conversation ability or complete A1 coverage. Listening/reading need their own comprehension evidence.

## Minimum authored learning pack

One topic may contain several short lessons; screen count and lesson count remain design decisions. The minimum pack contains:

- One reviewed short model introduction in text and reference audio, with three factual comprehension questions per modality. Use different equivalent examples so reading does not supply listening answers.
- Five reviewed useful word/phrase senses with meaning examples and productive recall cues that do not reveal the target. Natural equivalent wording is accepted in communication; a specific-target recall result is recorded separately.
- Five controlled grammar items with reviewed answer alternatives, a short explanation and a retry path.
- One guided introduction task, one repeatable independent baseline task, and at least two reserved related transfer prompts with unseen follow-ups. Transfer changes audience/context while preserving A1 demands and the three-fact outcome; it does not introduce new prerequisites.
- A versioned rubric, anchor examples, a learner self-review checklist and delayed recall tasks. Baseline and transfer material is separated from practice content.

All assets require original/permitted provenance and qualified editorial review under existing publication gates. AI variations are optional and cannot replace this reviewed offline pack or populate reserved assessments without comparability review.

## Learning sequence and completion

1. Offer an optional independent baseline before teaching. Keep it separate from the friendly first-recording onboarding; declining assessment never blocks practice.
2. Explain the practical goal; hear/read the model and check comprehension.
3. Interleave short grammar teaching, controlled practice and recall of the five useful phrases before answer reveal.
4. Give a guided speaking task using the learner's chosen details. Allow replay and self-review offline.
5. When requested and supported, provide afterward online feedback, normally up to two prioritized actionable improvements, each grounded in the attempt. Correct recognition before final text coaching. If no correction is warranted, say so rather than manufacturing two errors.
6. Offer a retry with less support. Preserve learner choice; a supported retry is practice evidence, not an independent assessment.
7. Revisit recall later and offer an unfamiliar related task. Failed/difficult items return to targeted teaching or practice with an explanation of why.

Core topic completion requires attempting all six comprehension questions, five grammar items and five productive recall cues; reviewing corrections/model answers for missed items; and completing one guided introduction attempt plus self-review. Incorrect answers do not prevent completion after correction/self-review. Quiet text practice is an equivalent completion route and is labeled text evidence; microphone denial or declining speaking leaves speech unassessed. A skipped core activity stays unfinished until a comparable replacement is attempted. Audio/text saving remains independently opt-in; completion does not require saving private payloads, online feedback, a successful assessment, gold or a provider key. Free explanations, corrective feedback, accessibility and retries remain available under the existing optional paid-hint policy.

## Evidence distinctions

Keep participation/completion, recognition, unaided recall, productive use, delayed retention and transfer as separate evidence. Recognition or answer exposure never establishes independent recall. A typed answer cannot establish speech intelligibility. An assisted response is valid practice but cannot pass an independent assessment. Missing, declined or deleted evidence is unavailable, never an inferred failure or success. Only explicit rated vocabulary reviews advance scheduling initially; these lesson/pilot checks do not silently create review credits.

## Initial assessment criteria

Assessment tasks use the same preparation allowance, prompt support, follow-up delivery/repetition rules and modality at baseline and comparison. The pilot protocol freezes these conditions before baseline. Learners can choose a supported mode, but its results remain assisted and are not compared as independent performance. Natural pauses, accent and speed alone are not errors.

| Dimension | 0 — not yet demonstrated | 1 — partly demonstrated | 2 — demonstrated independently |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task success | No relevant fact conveyed | One/two facts conveyed, or follow-up not answered meaningfully | Three relevant facts and a meaningful answer to one related follow-up |
| Vocabulary retrieval/use | Target meanings cannot be expressed | Some meanings expressed; assistance needed or major breakdown remains | Necessary meanings expressed appropriately without lexical hints; paraphrase allowed |
| Target grammar | Target structures usually prevent intended meaning | Some target errors, but intended meaning remains recoverable | Target structures generally controlled across the available opportunities |
| Intelligibility | Listener cannot recover most intended meaning | Listener recovers meaning with some effort/repetition | Listener readily understands the message; retained accent is acceptable |

Record each dimension separately, with an example and assistance status; do not convert the rubric into a single English-proficiency score. If the sample provides no opportunity to judge a dimension, mark it not assessed. Grammar correctness is judged on the learner-confirmed transcript where available; intelligibility requires actual audio and human listening or a separately validated audio capability. Self-review is not qualified assessment.

Initial independent task success requires task success = 2, vocabulary retrieval/use = 2, grammar at least 1 and intelligibility at least 1, with all dimensions assessable and no hints/model/corrections during the task. This is a local topic criterion, not general mastery. Reserve the stronger label "retained and transferred" for successful unfamiliar-task evidence and delayed productive recall; do not automatically award an improvement badge from one successful recording.

| Check | Initial pilot criterion | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading and listening | At least 2/3 correct first responses in each modality without answers exposed | Separate bounded comprehension evidence; corrections/retries are practice |
| Controlled grammar | At least 4/5 correct unaided first responses | Controlled knowledge, not spontaneous speaking mastery |
| Delayed productive recall | At least 4/5 selected target senses retrieved appropriately in an original phrase/sentence, unaided, at both delayed checks | Retention evidence; specific-target recall and communicative paraphrase reported separately |
| Related unfamiliar speaking task | Meets independent task criterion with a reserved prompt and follow-up | Transfer evidence under reviewed comparable conditions |
| Within-learner improvement | At least one rubric dimension rises by one level on a comparable unfamiliar task, task success does not decline, and delayed recall criterion is met | Initial signal requiring human review; not causal proof |

The productive recall checks occur around day 7 (±2 days) and day 28 (±3 days), measured from initial teaching. Record actual elapsed time and intervening practice; do not call this retention without practice. Out-of-window checks remain useful but do not pass the specified timing gate. Baseline ceiling results show maintenance/transfer when successful, not invented improvement; an unsuccessful/missing baseline cannot establish change. Educator/SQA must review anchor examples and these thresholds before the pilot, version changes prospectively, and never lower targets after seeing results to manufacture success.

## Adaptation and engagement requirements

Recommendations must name the observed difficulty and offer an appropriate next action: retrieval trouble → recall/paraphrase; recurring target grammar error → explanation and focused practice; difficulty with follow-ups → new questions and adjustable preparation; speech clarity concern → reference listening/replay and supported audio practice. Show a plain-language reason and allow a different choice. Insufficient evidence falls back to learner goals and due reviews, not a fabricated diagnosis or CEFR placement.

XP/gold/streaks reward eligible participation under existing deduplication rules. Progress displays separate skill evidence, support used and assessment availability; confidence is learner-reported. No reward, leaderboard, shorter pause target or mandatory accent imitation substitutes for communication evidence.

## Validation and expansion gate

Run the [Introductions acceptance protocol](../testing/introductions-learning-acceptance.md) through [SC-T043](../development-plan/tickets/SC-T043.md) before expanding into a small course. An owner pilot can reveal usefulness and learning signals; it cannot establish effectiveness for a population or superiority to another app. Broader claims require a suitable comparison study. Overall public-release A1–C2 requirements remain unchanged.

## Research basis and limits

The adopted design draws on research supporting [spaced language practice](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12479), research reviewing [oral corrective feedback](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/abs/oral-corrective-feedback-in-second-language-classrooms/B33FC71C12A1317DCA0CE704B7B0BC00), and the [CEFR emphasis on intelligibility](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/phonological-competence). These sources do not validate this app, the exact five-item pack, rubric thresholds or pilot duration. Those are operational starting criteria to review and test.

## English Foundations connection — adopted 2026-10-19

Apply the [INTRO-01 foundation mapping](../../../product-specification/english-foundations.md#first-connection-intro-01): EF-01-NOUN-01/EF-01-PRON-01, EF-02-SP-01, EF-03-BE-01 and EF-03-PRES-01. Teach noun/pronoun roles, subject versus predicate, be agreement and simple-present agreement through reviewed examples. Allocate the existing five controlled grammar items as two be, two simple-present agreement and one auxiliary question/negative. An optional five-item concept check records terminology understanding separately; it is not a new core completion requirement or condition for communication success.

Keep the three-fact self-introduction/follow-up unchanged. Use a separate reviewed fictional-colleague task if eliciting third-person agreement; insufficient target opportunities mean not assessed. Initial learning checks and delayed grammar-use evidence follow [EF acceptance](../testing/english-foundations-acceptance.md), separately from the existing five-sense vocabulary recall and IL communication rubric. Full objects/complements, phrase/clause types and progressive grammar are later coverage, not prerequisites imposed on this first topic.

## Specialized practice paths — adopted 2026-10-19

Provide reviewed Teacher (explain/model/guided-to-independent), Grammarian (agreement/construction then contextual use) and Journalist (relevant questions/follow-up/summary) practice paths under [the trainer contract](specialized-trainers.md). These share INTRO-01/EF objectives and preserve prior evidence. They are optional alternative/supplemental practice paths, not three extra mandatory sessions. Keep the six comprehension questions, five grammar items, five vocabulary cues, guided attempt/self-review and independent three-fact/follow-up assessment unchanged.

Specialty questioning or source-summary tasks record separate objectives and reviewed opportunities; they cannot silently replace the fixed INTRO-01 baseline or imply unassessed listening ability. SC-T018 authors the bounded packs, SC-T020/SC-T021 connect entry/return/progress, and SC-T043 checks actual method differentiation and relevant [TR criteria](../testing/specialized-trainer-acceptance.md). All seven roles remain required overall, with later delivery via SC-T044.

## Coach-independent required lesson clarification

All seven coaches present the same reviewed INTRO-01 required teaching, sequence, difficulty, core activities and assessment. Earlier Teacher/Grammarian/Journalist "paths" refer to separate optional specialist practice, never alternate required CEFR training. Their packs may use different activities outside the course; entering one is explicit and returns to the saved course position. Presentation-only switching preserves the fixed test; substantive assistance/task changes are tracked separately. See [course/practice rules](learning-path-and-practice.md) and CP-QA01–03/08.
