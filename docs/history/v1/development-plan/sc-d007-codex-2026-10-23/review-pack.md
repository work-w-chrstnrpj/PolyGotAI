## Greet someone — SC-T074

Lesson `a1-greetings` · objective `GREET-01` · content version `3`.

A greeting opens a conversation. Hello works at many times. Good morning is for the morning; good evening is for meeting someone in the evening. Good night usually ends an evening conversation. In I am fine, I is a subject pronoun, am is a linking verb, and fine describes the subject. A question with be puts the verb before the subject: Are you ready? Thank you and goodbye help close an exchange.

Foundation allocation: `EF-01-ADJ-01`, `EF-01-CONJ-01`, `EF-01-INTERJ-01`, `EF-01-PRON-01`, `EF-02-SP-01`, `EF-03-BE-01`, `EF-04-CLAUSE-01`.

### Concept checks — editorial supplement

1. In Hello, I am Ana, which word is a greeting? Answer/example: Hello.
2. In I am fine, which word names the speaker without using a name? Answer/example: I; it is a subject pronoun.
3. In I am fine, what is the complete predicate? Answer/example: am fine; fine is a subject complement, not an object.
4. Why is Are you ready? a question rather than You are ready? in the neutral written pattern? Answer/example: Are comes before the subject you; the sentence ends with a question mark.
5. Which usually closes an evening visit: Good evening or Good night? Answer/example: Good night; Good evening normally greets someone.

### Pronunciation self-review

Replay the greeting clip. Notice the stress in MORning and THANK you. Record your own greeting if you wish; compare whether the greeting and reply can be understood. Text alone cannot show pronunciation.

### Workplace connection

Greet a receptionist and ask for repetition: Could you say that again, please?

## Spell your name — SC-T076

Lesson `a1-alphabet` · objective `SPELL-01` · content version `3`.

English letters have names used when spelling. A written name begins with a capital letter. In My name is Ben, name is a noun and my shows whose name it is. Is links the subject my name to Ben. Use How do you spell it? to request letters and Please repeat that to repair a misunderstanding. Letter names and the sounds inside a word are different.

Foundation allocation: `EF-01-DET-01`, `EF-01-NOUN-01`, `EF-02-BOUND-01`, `EF-02-IMP-01`, `EF-02-SP-01`, `EF-03-BE-01`, `EF-03-PRES-01`.

### Concept checks — editorial supplement

1. In My name is Ben, which word is a common noun? Answer/example: name; Ben is a proper noun.
2. Does my replace the noun name, or come before it? Answer/example: It comes before name; here it is a possessive determiner.
3. In My name is Ben, is Ben an object? Answer/example: No. It is the subject complement after linking verb is.
4. What punctuation ends How do you spell it? Answer/example: A question mark.
5. Do the letter name B and the sound at the start of Ben have to be identical? Answer/example: No. A letter name is different from the sound represented in a word.

### Pronunciation self-review

Listen to A, N, A in the clip. Pause between the letter names. If recording, ask whether the separate letters can be heard; do not infer sound accuracy from typed spelling.

### Workplace connection

## Use numbers — SC-T077

Lesson `a1-numbers` · objective `NUMBER-01` · content version `3`.

Use one with a singular count noun and two or more with a plural: one ticket, two tickets. How many asks about a count; How much asks about a price. In I need two tickets, I is the subject, need two tickets is the predicate, and two tickets is the direct object. The noun phrase two tickets has the head tickets. Give invented prices rather than personal financial details.

Foundation allocation: `EF-01-DET-01`, `EF-01-NOUN-NUM-01`, `EF-02-OBJ-01`, `EF-03-BE-01`, `EF-03-PRES-01`, `EF-04-NP-01`.

### Concept checks — editorial supplement

1. In I need two tickets, what is the subject? Answer/example: I.
2. In I need two tickets, what is the direct object? Answer/example: two tickets; it is what is needed.
3. What is the head noun in two tickets? Answer/example: tickets; two modifies its quantity.
4. Which asks about quantity: how many or how much? Answer/example: How many, with a count noun.
5. Why does one ticket have no plural s? Answer/example: One refers to a single countable item.

## Say the day and the time — SC-T078

Lesson `a1-dates` · objective `DATE-01` · content version `3`.

Use on with a named day, at with a clock time, and in with a month: on Friday, at three, in March. These are prepositional phrases that add time information to a clause. In The class starts at nine, The class is the subject and starts at nine is the predicate. English date orders differ; say the month in words to avoid ambiguity. A date, an hour, and a month are different kinds of information.

Foundation allocation: `EF-01-PREP-01`, `EF-02-SP-01`, `EF-03-BE-01`, `EF-03-PRES-01`, `EF-04-PP-01`.

### Concept checks — editorial supplement

1. Which phrase gives a day in on Friday at three? Answer/example: on Friday.
2. Which phrase gives a clock time in on Friday at three? Answer/example: at three.
3. In The class starts at nine, what is the predicate? Answer/example: starts at nine.
4. In at nine, what type of word is at? Answer/example: A preposition.
5. Why can writing 04/05 without context confuse someone? Answer/example: Different date conventions can mean April 5 or May 4; writing the month name helps.

### Pronunciation self-review

Listen for the stressed day and hour in the recording. Pause between the day and the time when needed. A clear text date is not evidence of spoken stress.

### Workplace connection

Confirm a fictional meeting day and time without using a private calendar.

## Talk about the weather — SC-T086
